The [[Ionisation|Ionising]] Radiation (Medical Exposures) Regulations (IR(ME)R) are the regulations that stop us from using total body irradiation to treat all patients.  This is because they are the regulations that govern the use of ionising radiation on patients—see [[IRR]] for staff.

There are 4 key Duty holders underneath the framework lain out within IR(ME)R:
- Employer-HUTH
- Referrer- Doctor 1
- Practitioner-Doctor 2
- Operator- Me : ) (and others)

# Operator
An operator is anyone that has a practical role in a medical exposure.

This radiographers who set-up and finally complete the exposure; physicist, who optimise exposures and maintain equipment; clinicians, how report on medical images or contour tumour targets; and many others.
## Medical Physics Experts
>They are legally called experts, isn't that cool?

Medical Physics Experts (MPEs) are a special class of operator who have recognition from the RPA-2000 board (who also recognise [[IRR#Radiation Protection Advisors|Radiation Protection Advisors]]) as well as their local employer.  They are required by law to be involved in radiation exposures to varying degrees.
- Closely involved in every [[Radiotherapy]] practice (other than standardised nuclear medicine)
- Involved in [[Nuclear medicine]], high-[[Dose]] interventional, and high-dose [[CT]]
- involved as appropriate for consultation of optimisation of all other practices
- Give advice on:
	- Dosimetry and quality control matters relating to rad prot concern exposures.
	- Measurements of deserved dose
	- Medical Radiological Equipmetn
- Contribute to:
	- Optimisation and DRLs
	- Creating and performing quality assurance
	- Acceptence testing
	- Technical preperation of equipment
	- surveillance of installations
	- analysis of mistaken exposures
	- selection of equipment to perform measurements
	- training of staff
	- provision of advice
- Communicate to RPAs and RWAs.
