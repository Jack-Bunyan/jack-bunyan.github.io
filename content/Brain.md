# Cerebrum
![[Cerebrum image.png]]
This is the main structure of the brain and consists of 2 hemisphere connected via the corpus callosum.  The surface of the cerebrum-known as the cortex- has convultions (gryi) and crevices (sulci).  The four regions of the brain are categorised as follows.
- Frontal
	- Decision-making and problem solving
	- Motor control
	- Speech
	- Personality (pre-frontal cortex)
- Parietal
	- Sensory
	- Spatial processing
- Temporal
	- Auditory and language
	- Memory
- Occipital
	- Visual processing

The tissues of the brain can be categorised as either
- Grey matter
	- Neuronal bodies
	- Processers
- White Matter
	- Myelianted axons
	- Wiring
- Deep grey

The corpus callosum joins the two hemispheres of the brain, allowing them to communicate.  It is mainly constructed of axons, with two portions known a the genu (anterior) and splenium (posterior).

# [[On Blood|Blood]]
The most important feature of the brain's arterial structure is the circle of willis cause its funny.
![[circle of willis uni.png]]

The ventricular system of the brain contains CNS, the walls of these ventricles contain choroid plexus that produces new CSF, asymmetry of the ventricle system may indicate many pathologies.  Shrinking of the ventricles may be a result of tumour growth, while enlargement of the ventricles increasing in size may indicate hydrocephalus.  The brain's venous supply drains through the sinuses.
![[blood drainage.png]]

The blood brain barrier maintains selective diffusion of molecules from the blood into the brain.  While glucose and oxygen can perfuse easily, larger molecules require specific active transporters, this prevents toxins and disease getting into the brain.  Contrast enhancement may indicate contrast enhancement, whilst radiopharmaceutical distribution is affected by the integrity of the barrier.
