> 💡 This scan looks at airflow and blood flow distribution in the [[lungs]].

![[DTPA.png]]

According to [current guidance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6039809/) diagnosis/imaging of pulmonary embolisms is current done using [[CTPA]]; however, lung VQ imaging is still done for 3 contra-indications:
- Young women: Lung VQ has signficiantly lower breast dose
- Contrast issues: allergy or renal failure
- Size

## Patient Preparation

- Remove any metal objects from over patient’s chest, including [[ECG]] leads and necklaces.
- Familiarise patient with inhalation equipment, (see specific instructions [here](https://app.notion.com/p/Lung-VQ-1fb9796f42ba4bb4958d3a36f15e8139?pvs=21)).
- Explain reasoning for [[PPE]] and do a dummy run to ensure patient is creating seal with mouth and they understand procedure.

## Procedure

### Ventilation

- SmartVent kit used (see pictures):
    - Solution is put into **generator (nebuliser).**
    - Generator converts solution from liquid into aerosol (see [Extras](https://app.notion.com/p/Lung-VQ-1fb9796f42ba4bb4958d3a36f15e8139?pvs=21))
    - The aerosol collects in the generator connector and travels through the output tube when the patient breaths in.
    - All breathing by patient goes through the mouthpiece, face mask is also available if this is difficult.
        - Face mask leads to contamination of patient’s face.

![[Charlie-Docs/Charlie_Assets/Lung VQ kit.png|330]]![[Lung VQ kit 2.png|330]]
*SmartVent components, HEPA filter is attached to the generator to prevent any of the gas from escaping into the atmosphere*

- ~1000MBq of [[Tc99m]] DTPA drawn up in 1ml solution is added to the generator.
- With the mouthpiece in patient’s mouth (and nose clip on), the generator is turned on and patient breathes for 30-60s until becomes difficult.
- Remove nose clip then mouthpiece after a breath or two.
- Measure cps of lungs from posterior of patient.
    - Aiming for 13,000cps on Berthold LB124 (S/N 9777).
    - Aiming for 15,500 cps on Berthold LB124 (10-11567).
> This equates to ~20MBq in the lungs.
- Once this is achieved, transport patient to camera room for scanning.
### Perfusion

- Injection of 99mTC-labelled human albumin (MAA) on camera and imaged immediately after.
    - Diameter of 15-100µm are nearly universally used.
    - This leads to microembolisation of pulmonary precapillary arterioles and capillaries.
        - As MAA is a blood product, certain religions may refuse the injection.
- Take count rate from posterior of patient to note on form.
- Image as protocol.


## Normal Results

- Ventilation and perfusion images match with no photopenia on either.
- Higher counts towards posterior/inferior of lungs due to gravity.
![[Lung VQ scan images.png]]

## What shows up?

- Gaps missing in ventilation but normal perfusion - is usually a sign of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) but can also be caused by:
    - Pulmonary infections such as pneumonia
    - Alveolar collapse
    - Asthma
    - Eisenmenger syndrome
- Normal ventilation but gaps missing in perfusion - is usually a sign of pulmonary embolisms (PE) (blood clots) but can also be caused by:
    - Cardiovascular shock
    - Emphysema
- Similar defects in both - considered a normal scan.

## Extras

- Pulmocis works by blocking vessels and anchoring itself in position. Patients with pulmonary hypertension already exhibit reduced oxygen transfer in the lungs.
    - For this reason, a reduced number of particles are needed in their injection in order to reduce the risk of internal suffocation.

### How does the generator work?

- The generator is an electronic micropump which produces liquid aerosol with an average size of 1.32µm.

![[Lung Image for VQ.png]]
- In the centre of the generator, there is a dome-shaped aperture plate containing >1000 holes (this dome is surrounded by a vibrational element).
- When energy is applied, aperture plate vibrates at over 125,000 times/second, this causes the plate to act as a micropump - pulling liquid through the holes, forming consistently sized droplets.

### Wiki Procedure

1. Remove all non-essential objects from the room.
2. Ensure the patient is lying on a trolley.
3. Using a single use mouth piece with nose clip or face mask, practice the required breathing with the patient.
4. Ensure a good lip or tight face seal is achieved.
5. Make sure the patient understands to breathe normally.
6. Instruct the patient to raise their hand if they have difficulties breathing during the administration.
7. Practice these last two steps with the patient if necessary.
8. During this practice assess the likely patient compliance with instructions.
9. Explain the procedure to the patient, including that they will be required to take at least three breaths once the nebuliser is turned off before removing the mouth piece or face mask.
10. Put on disposable gloves, a long-sleeved gown, a mob cap and a theatre mask.
11. Transfer 800-1000MBq of DTPA in 1 ml into the nebuliser chamber for the first patient on each imaging day. If additional patients are to be imaged on the same day, add an additional 500MBq of DTPA in 0.5ml, prior to each patient. NOTE: To prevent damage, when filling the chamber if a needle is used it must not touch the base of the chamber.
12. Position the nebulising equipment so that members of staff are not in the stream of any potential airborne contamination.
13. Instruct the patient to hold the administration tubing and attach it to the nebuliser system.
14. Assist the patient to position the mouth piece or face mask ensuring a good lip or tight face seal is achieved.
15. If the mouth piece is being used, place the nose clip on the patient.
16. Turn the nebuliser on and ask the patient to take a couple of deep breaths and then to breathe in and out normally through the mouthpiece or mask.
17. After 1 minute of inhalation administration, turn off the nebuliser and encourage the patient to take at least three breaths before removing the nose clip and mouth piece or face mask.

2. Alveolar permeability
3. Lung quantification pre-surgery
