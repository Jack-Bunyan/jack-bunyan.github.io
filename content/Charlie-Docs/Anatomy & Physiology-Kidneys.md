The kidneys are two bean shaped organs below the liver, with the liver itself pushing the right hand kidney to be slight lower than the left.  They are typically surrounded by perirenal fat providing a protective layer.

## Kidney Structure
![[Kidney-Physical-Features.png]]
- **Renal hilum** - entry and exit site for vessels, nerves, lymphatics and ureters.
- **Renal cortex** - outer region of the kidney, containing various [[blood]] vessels and most importantly, nephrons.
- **Renal medulla** - inner region of the kidney containing 5-8 renal pyramids which are separated by renal columns.
    - urine is created in these pyramids and terminates in the renal papilla. it is then drained from here into a collecting pool, minor calyx. several of these connect into the major calyx and then into the ureter to travel to the bladder.

## Nephron

- Nephrons are the functional unit within the kidneys.
    - there are an estimated 1.3 million nephrons within each kidney
    - their primary function is to filter blood
        - they also control blood pressure through the production of renin
        - and red blood cell production via the hormone erythropoietin
        - and calcium absorption via conversion of calcidiol into calcitriol

![[Nephron-Structure.png|287]]![[Glomerules capsule figure.png]]
- Each nephron has a specialised networks of ducts called a tubule.
    - this extends to the Bowman’s capsule, where filtered fluid from the glomerulus is collected
- The glomerulus is fed by the afferent arteriole
    - blood is filtered by the glomerulus producing filtrate
- This filtrate travels to the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT), the loop of Henle and distal convoluted tubule (DCT).
- This then exits the nephron into the common collecting ducts, renal pyramids which are shared by many nephrons.
- Blood exits the nephron through the peritubular capillaries, leaving through the efferent arteriole.

## Glomerular Filtration

- The glomerulus is simply a filter, so anything in the plasma that can fit through the filtration membrane will make it’s way into the filtrate.
    - this means the filtrate is lacking cells and large [[proteins]].
- This filtrate is highly modified along its journey through the nephron by tubular reabsorption and tubular secretion.
![[Glomerular Filtration.png]]
## Tubular Reabsorption

- As the filtrate travels along the nephron tubule, the lining cells selectively, and actively, moves substances from the filtrate and back into blood supply.
    - this includes [[water]], sodium, chloride, [[glucose]] and even amino acids
        - these cells are so efficient so up to 99% of water and important ions are reabsorped into the bloodstream
![[Kidney Reabsorption.png]]
## Tubular Secretion

- This mostly occurs in the PCT and DCT where substances are moved from the peritubular capillary.
    - this is mostly substances that are too large to be filtered (e.g., antibiotics, toxins)
    - but also substances that are in excess (e.g., H+, K+)
- The substances are secreted into the tubule to leave the body as a component of urine.

# Imaging
Dynamic scans as done with [[Tc99m-MAG3]] or [[Tc99m#Tc99m-DTPA|Tc99m-DTPA]] assess renal perfusion, function and drainage of the kidneys.

Static Cortical Imaging performed using [[Tc99m-DMSA]], this binds to the proximal tubules, evaluation which areas of the renal parenchyma are functional.

Though not an imaging modality the function of the kidneys filtration rate can be evaulated using GFR measurement, this is done using either [[Cr51-EDTA]] or [[Tc99m#Tc99m-DTPA|Tc99m-DTPA]].
