- The pharmaceutical used is a variant of iminodiacetic acid (IDA) which is tightly bound to albumin.
	- IDA-bound-albumin follows the bilirubin metabolic pathway, meaning they are extracted and excreted as if they were bilirubin.

> Albumin is a protein synthesised in the liver which assists in the transport of small molecules in the bloodstream

- Bilirubin metabolism can be summarised into four steps ([Source](https://www.sciencedirect.com/sdfe/pdf/download/eid/3-s2.0-B9781416032564500156/first-page-pdf)):
	1) Production - IDA-bound-albumin is injected intravenously, 80% of the body’s bilirubin is produced in the bone marrow from the breakdown of haemoglobin
	2) Hepatocyte Uptake - IDA/bilirubin-bound-albumin is taken up to the hepatocytes within the liver
		- Hepatocytes are the functional cells in the liver, responsible for:
			- carbohydrate, lipid and protein metabolism
			- detoxification
			- immune cell activation to maintain liver homeostasis
	3) Conjugation - bilirubin is conjugated using microsomal enzymes forming two water-soluble compounds
		- Bilirubin monoglucuronide (15%)
		- Bilirubin diglucuronide (85%)
	4) Excretion - IDA/conjugated bilirubin is excreted into the bile canaliculus and then may be stored in the gallbladder
		- This is the rate-limiting step in this process

> **Conjugation** \[of bacteria or unicellular organisms] is where they are temporarily united in order to exchange genetic material.  Though the tracer is not conjugated this is an important step in bilirubin metabolism.

# Anatomy and Physiology


The biliary system refers to bile production, secretion and storage via the liver, gallbladder and bile ducts. Bile ducts are categorised as either intrahepatic or extrahepatic:

![[Bile Ducts CB.png|697]]

| During fasting, the gallbladder fills up with bile due to the resistance caused with the Sphincter of Oddi being closed. | During digestion, the gallbladder contracts and the Sphincter of Oddi relaxes to allow bile to enter the duodenum to aide in digestion and absorption of fats. |

- Bile is secreted in the liver by hepatocytes.
- Bile is initially alkaline (pH 8.20) but becomes altered by epithelial cells in the biliary tract and once in the gallbladder, it becomes acidic (pH 5.18 to 6.00) and concentrated.
- The Pancreas’ main role is the production of enzymes to aide in digestion. It produces:
	- Lipase
		- breaks down fats into fatty acids
	- Amylase
		- breaks down starch into sugar
	- Protease
		- breaks down proteins into smaller polypeptides or single amino acids
- When digestion begins cholecystokinin (CCK), a digestion hormone, alerts the Sphincter of Oddi to relax and the gallbladder to contract causing influx of bile into the duodenum allowing for digestion of food.
# Patient Preparation
- Adults must fast for a minimum of 2 hours but preferably 6 hours before the appointment time.
	- Ideal fasting time is 3-4 hours as food stimulates natural release of CCK resulting in gallbladder contraction thus potentially restricting radioactive tracer entry
- Patients fasting for longer than 24 h (including those on total parenteral nutrition), can cause the gallbladder not to fill with radiotracer within the normally expected time frame. In these cases, pre-treatment with sincalide might be necessary.
	- Sincalide is administered to empty the gall bladder and allow IDA to enter
		- If this is not done, a false-positive could be obtained
- For patients being investigated for suspected biliary atresia, pre-treat with 5mg/kg/day of Phenobarbitone for 5 days before the appointment.
	- Phenobarbitone is primarily used to treat seizures, however it is used in this case to relax gut muscles in preparation for the test
- For patients being investigated for suspected Sphincter of Oddi Dysfunction it may be necessary to pre-treat with sincalide over 10 minutes. The infusion should be completed 15 minutes prior to radiopharmaceutical administration (i.e. the radiopharmaceutical is administered 15 minutes after the end of the sincalide infusion).
	- See Sphincter of Oddi Dysfunction
- For patients undergoing Calogen studies, check the patient is not allergic to peanuts. If they are, do not proceed with the study without permission from the ARSAC licence holder.
	- Calogen is not suitable for those with galactosaemia, a condition meaning the body’s enzymes have an issue breaking down the sugar galactose

# Sphincter of Oddi Dysfunction
![[Oddi Sphincter HDA.png]]
*Hepatobiliary system anatomy - note Sphincter of Oddi and Ampulla of Vater ([Source](https://muschealth.org/medical-services/ddc/patients/digestive-diseases/pancreas/sphincter-of-oddi-dysfunction)).*

- The sphincter of Oddi is the muscular valve surrounding the exit of the bile duct and pancreatic duct into the duodenum. The sphincter is normally closed, only opening in response to a meal so that digestive juices can enter the duodenum and mix with food for digestion.

> Sphincter of Oddi (SOD) dysfunction is a clinical syndrome described when the flow of pancreatic enzymes or biliary excretions are obstructed from entering the duodenum through the sphincter of Oddi. It can be caused by SOD stenosis (narrowing of the SOD) or SOD dyskinesia (smooth muscles disturbances of the SOD). ([Source](https://radiopaedia.org/cases/sphincter-of-oddi-dysfunction-type-2-1?lang=us))

- Approximately 10-20% of patients who have had a cholecystectomy (gallbladder removed) for chronic cholecystitis do develop recurrent abdominal pain. The most common biliary causes include retained or recurrent biliary duct stones, inflammatory stricture, and, less commonly, sphincter of Oddi dysfunction.

> Sphincter of Oddi dysfunction is the cause in approximately 10% of patients with post-cholecystectomy pain.

SOD may be cause by one of two things

|            | Papillary Stenosis                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Biliary Dyskinesia                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Definition | a condition where a sphincter opening mechanism is disturbed (in this context, it is mostly caused by a fixed obstruction, i.e., gallstone)                                                                               | a motility disorder affecting the muscles in the biliary system (in this context, with the Sphincter of Oddi not opening when it should. a backlog of bile causes pressure within the system causing the gallbladder to fill whilst producing bile, causing inflammation of the gallbladder, cholecystitis) |
| Treatment  | this is the more straightforward form of treatment - diagnosed and treated with anatomical imaging and [ERCP](https://www.guysandstthomas.nhs.uk/health-information/ercp-endoscopic-retrograde-cholangio-pancreatography) | endoscopic biliary sphincterotomy (this is a procedure combined with ERCP, and an incision is made in the Sphincter of Oddi, allowing flow of bile into the duodenum. this is often performed for assumed sphincter dysfunction)                                                                            |
**How do we scan for it**
The gold standard for SOD diagnosis is manometry (inserting a catheter down the patient’s nose that has sensors recording nerve and muscle reactions). This is done while eating to assess for dyskinesia. However, this is an invasive procedure that carries with it a substantial risk of inducing pancreatitis ([Source](https://jnm.snmjournals.org/content/61/supplement_1/1454)).

## How does Sincilade work?

- Sincalide is a synthetic octapeptide of cholecystokinin.
- It is used for evaluation of the gallbladder ejection fraction and in Sphincter of Oddi dysfunction tests.
- It is injected intravenously slowly (over 10 minutes) as a bolus injection can cause abdominal discomfort and less complete gallbladder emptying due to sphincter spasming.
- Sincalide imitates CCK causing gallbladder contraction, relaxing of the Sphincter of Oddi, increasing bile secretion and bowel motility.
	- In normal patients, there is maximum gallbladder contraction 5-15 minutes post slow administration
