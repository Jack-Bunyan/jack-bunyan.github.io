Bones are a core aspect of the body, providing both the structure that keeps us standing and allows us to move, while also providing protection to the squishy parts.  They also have a core role in some physiological aspects such as the production of [[On Blood#White Blood Cells|White Blood Cells]] for the immune system.

There are 5 main categories of bone:
- Long bone: providing the main structure to the body e.g. femur
- Short Bones: Allow for small manipulation e.g. fingers and toes
- Flat Bones: Protective bone making up the skull
- Irregular: Spine
- Sesamoid: very small bones such as the kneecap

## Long Bone Structure
![[Long Bone Structure.png]]
The Long bone itself has 2 main aspects known as the Epiphysis and Diaphysis, with an intermediate stage called the Metaphysis and the surface membrane known as the Periosteum.

The Epiphysis contains the spongy cancellous bone and acts between joints to distribute loads, whilst the diaphysis has a greater amount of cortical bone, maintaining the strength of the bone.  The intermediate Metaphysis stage is also where the bone growth occurs in children, whilst the periosteum membrane provides nutrition to the bone, allowing for growth and repair.

# Bone Tissues

There are two kinds of bone tissue that make up the human skeleton.  Compact (cortical) bone provides the structural rigidity to the system, surrounding less dense Cancellous (Trabeccular) bone filled with red [[Bone#Marrow|Marrow]].

## Compact


![[Osteon-Structure.png]]
Compact bone within the body is strongly calcified, made up of small Osteon units, these contain are concentric structures forming along the length of the bone.  The extracellular Lamellae within this provide the structural strength to the system containing both organic and non-organic ([[Calcium]]) components, whilst the osteocytes are the actual bone cells. 
These Osteons are arranged surrounding the Haversian canals, which provide the blood flow and nutrients to the structure.  The canaliculi that branch of of this allow the nutrients of the canals to reach all of the cells within the osteon structure.
![[Osteoclasts_figure.png]]![[OsteoBlasts_figure.png]]The actual Osteoclasts and -blasts are the core cells behind the skeletal systems growth and repair.  Contrary to their naming system, the Osteoclasts are responsible for the breakdown of the lacunae, whereas the blasts are responsible for the buildup of new bones.

>The creation of new bones uses [[Calcium]] and [[Whole Body Bone|HA crystals]], uptake is expected at the ends of bones where joints will wear, however some pathologies such as [[Oncology#Metastasis|Metastasis]], making it useful for #Nuclear_Medicine Diagnostics and [[Radium|Therapy]].

## Marrow

The bone marrow has a vital role in the body, with the Red Marrow performing [[On Blood#Haematopoesis|Haematopoesis]], producing red and white blood cells as well as platelets.  Ensuring that the body has adequate blood supply and the capability of fighting infection.  The yellow marrow in the body has the capability of converting to red marrow in times of need but typically acts as an energy store, composed majorly of adipose tissue.
# Related Pathologies
Osteoporosis increased osteoclastic activity resulting in elevated breakdown of cortical bone, results in lower density bone and increased fracture risk, identifiable via [[DEXA]] scanning or visible in [[CT]] scanning as cortical thinning.

Bone Metastasis can result in either increased or decreased bone density, with different cancers having altered probabilities of either, in either case this can be identified using [[FDG|PET]] imaging due to the increased metabolic activity.

Osteomyelitis is where an infection can trigger osteoclastic activity resulting is bone breakdown and marrow changes, early detection of this is possible via [[MRI]] before any radiographic changes are visible.
