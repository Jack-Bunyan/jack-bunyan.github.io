SABR is a treatment similar to [[SRS- UN 15-10-2026|SRS]], where rather than relying on healthy vs tumour cell recovery the intent of the treatment is to be ablative.  They are typically 3-5 fractions with [[Biologically Effective Dose|BED]] values greater than 100Gy, due to their high [[Dose]]/fractions.

SABR is less robust to random errors during treatment as the small number of fractions are unikely to cancel out.  This makes the immobilisation and treatment setups of these treatments very important, especially due to the typically small size of the legions treated and the high dose falloff meaning small movements can significantly compromise treatment quality.

SABR is currently approved for treatment for several primaries:
- [[Lungs]]
- [[Liver]]
- [[Prostate]]
- [[Pancreatic|Pancreas]]
With some metastatic sites also treated:
- Lung
- [[Bone|Bone]]
- [[Lymph]]
- [[Spine]]
- [[Adrenal]]
- Liver

SABR is a very clinically involved area, with it being required that any patient being treated with SABR is considered at an MDT.  In the UK for a centre to deliver SABR they must be approved by the UK SABR consortium, proving that they are capable of delivering sufficiently good treatments.

SABR gets to have a lot of fun imaging, with the many sites having different difficulties which lean towards different techniques.  Use of [[MRI]], [[PET]], and [[CT#4D|4DCT]] are very typical in order to delineate tumours and assess the motion of the tumour and local tissues.  E.g. spine treatments around the level of the diaphram require 4D to evaluate the varying [[On Particle Interactions#Cross-section|Attenuation]] of the tissue.

SABR is quite fun in that it allows for much higher hotspots than other sites, with lungs allowing for 140% (body 130%).
