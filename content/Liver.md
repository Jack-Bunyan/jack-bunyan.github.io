The liver is a large organ located inferior to the right lung, lying beneath the diagram.  It plays a part in the digestive process and the homeostasis of [[Blood]]; producing bile which aids in the breakdown of fats within the [[Digestive System#Small Intestine|Small Intestine]], the storage of vital vitamins, and temporary storage of glycogen to maintain blood sugar levels.

The liver contains 2 [[cells]] which are unique to the liver, these are the hepatocytes and biliary cells (cholangiocytes).  Hepatocytes make up 70-80% of the mass of the liver, and perform the two key physiological roles of the liver, bile production and blood homeostasis.  They are highly polarised cells which aid in the transport of molecules across cell barriers, filtering toxins from the blood as well as transporting nutrients back into them.

Biliary cells, on the other hand, are responsible for lining the bile ducts of the liver, both within (intrahepatic) and without (extreahepatic).  They have a vital role in the repair and immunologic aspects of the system.

# Imaging

There are many options that allow for the imaging of the liver.  As a soft tissue, [[ultrasound]] and [[MRI]] both provide excellent soft tissue contrast of the liver; ultrasound imaging can be used to evaluate the texture of liver tissue, and doppler imaging can provide flow information.  MRI can be used to characterise legions and fat depositions, as well as quantification of the iron content of the liver.  This can be done using [[MRI Relaxometry]] ([Example Images](https://radiopaedia.org/cases/mr-liver-iron-quantification-t2-relaxometry#image-8565881)).

Nuclear medicine can provide direct imaging of liver function through [[Hepatobiliary Iminodiacetic Acid (HIDA) Scan]]s, directly analysing the bile ducts.

# [[Oncology]]

Cancer can occur in both the liver itself, as well as the bile ducts.  With Liver cancer being the sixth most common cancer world-wide, with a long-term survival rate between 3 and 5%, being 2.4 times more common in men than women.  Common symptoms of liver cancer include, abdominal pain, weight loss, weakness, fullness and anorexia, abdominal swelling, jaundice, and vomiting; with physical signs such as hepatomelagy, hepatic bruit, ascites, spenomegaly, jaundice, wasting, and fever.

Due to it's use as the body's blood filtration system, many tumours metastasis to the liver, and tumours within the liver can infiltrate the local blood vessels.  The prognosis for patients depends strongly on the stage they are diagnosed at and underlying liver conditions.  40% of patients in the UK survive more than a year, with 15% surviving past 5.  4-year survival rates for each of the stages are 50, 35, 10, and 5% respectively.

T(umour) staging
- 0: No evidence of Primary tumour
- 1: Has not infiltrated the blood vessels of the liver
	- a: Single Tumour that is less than 2cm
	- b: Single tumour greater than 2cm
- 2: either
	- Single tumour that is more than 2cm and has infiltrated vessels
	- or, there are 2 or more tumours all less than 5cm
- 3: 2 or more tumours in the liver, at least one is greater than 5cm
- 4: either
	- Infiltrated a major blood vessel (portal or hepatic)
	- or, into organs close to the liver (-gallbladder)
	- or, through the lining of the abdomenal organs (visceral peritoneum)

N(odes) staging
- 0: No cancer cells in nearby lymph nodes
- 1: Cancer cells in nearby lymph nodes (advanced)

M(etastasis) staging
- 0: Has not spread to other areas
- 1: Has spread to other parts of the body e.g. lung, bones

While liver cancer is a frequent location for metastatic spread, being 25% of all metastatic tumours, being more common that primary hepatocellular tumours.  This is especially the case of gastrointestinal, [[Lungs|Lung]], [[Breast Cancer|Breast]], and [[Pancreatic]] tumours due to its rich blood supply.  While liver cancers frequently spread to the lungs, portal vein, and portal lymph nodes.

## Treatments

Radical treatments for liver cancer include resection, transplantation, ablation, and systemic methods.  Radiotherapy is only used as an ablative treatment for patients who are not suitable for surgery, with [[SABR]] being used for small tumours and Selective Internal Radiation Therapy [[SIRT]] for larger ones.  Radiotherapy is also used in later stage patients as a palliative measure.
