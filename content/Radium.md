# Radium-223 Therapy

>This is a molecular therapy used in the palliation of prostate metastasis.

![[Radium dichloride.png|662]]
Radium dichloride is a [[Radio-pharmaceutical]] that is used in the palliative treatment of [[Prostate]] metastatic cancer.  Unlike most metastatic tumours, which are typically osteoclastic, prostate metastasis are osteoclastic tumours, this means that they stimulate local bone growth increasing the uptake of calcium.  Radium can act as an analogue for calcium so can be used to target these growths as a therapeutic agent.

## Radium-223

![[Radium Decay Chain.png]]
Radium-223 is generated using a cyclotron, with a half-life of 11.43 days.  It has a decay chain that releases a variety of alpha and beta particles which produce localised therapeutic affects.

## Nice Guidance
This treatment is [reccomended by NICE](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta412/documents/final-appraisal-determination-document) for the treatment of hormone-relapsed prostate cancer, where bone metastases are resulting in pain, with no visceral spread.  A course of radium therapy costs approximately £24,240, however this brings the cost significantly down as part of a confidential patient access scheme.

## Dose
The prescribed activity of Radium to a patient is 55kBq/kg of body weight, prescribed by an oncologist, this is delivered every 4 weeks throughout the course of a treatment, typically receiving 6 fractions.
## Patient Preparation
Since Radium is a bone-seeking agent that will be proximal to the [[Bone#Marrow|Bone Marrow]] where the blood cells are produced, blood tests are a vital part of treatment.  These tests ensure that the treatment is not damaging the formation of blood cells which will have an impact on the immune system and oxygen transport.

|                                | Baseline Values         | Values Before Non-first Treatments |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Hb                             | >100g/L                 | >80g/L (stable)                    |
| Platelets                      | >100$\times$ 10$^9$/L   | >50$\times$ 10$^9$/L               |
| Neutrophils                    | >1.5 $\times$ 10 $^9$/L | >1 $\times$ 10 $^9$/L              |
| Serum Creatinine               | <1.5 $\times$ ULN       |                                    |
| Albumin                        | >25g/L                  |                                    |
| Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) | <2.5 $\times$ ULN       |                                    |
ULN-Upper limit of normal.
Creatinine and ALT can be overriden by the clinician.

When administering the radium it is important to avoid tissue-ing of the pharmaceutical, as an alpha-emitter it will deliver considerable localised dose if not dispersed within the blood.
