# Background
Was looking into trials to consider for [S-RP-S3-13](https://curriculumlibrary.nshcs.org.uk/stp/competency/S-RP-S3-1/13/) and found some [[Boron Neutron Capture Therapy|Weird Wacky Fun Ones]]; however, as these are a)expensive and b) very non-standard, meaning that they are unlikely to ever have widespread impact on the way we treat patients.

On the other hand, one kind of trial that has been very successful in the treatment of patients has been SBRT trials such as [PACE](https://www.icr.ac.uk/research-and-discoveries/centres-and-strategic-collaborations/clinical-trials-and-statistics-unit-icr-ctsu/detail/pace) and [STAMPEDE2](https://www.stampede2trial.org/).  The importance of these was to show that the treatment outcomes for hypofractionated treatments would not be worse than the the current treatment options (surgery for [[Lungs]] and [[Prostate Radiotherapy|Radical]] radiotherapy for prostate).

# Conversation
When comparing the overall dose of a treatment such as is done in trial situations to establish the importance of prescriptions the question is always "What is [[Dose]]?".  In typical radical treatments this is easier to establish as the dose to the PTV is relatively conformal.

In SABR patients the dose delivered to the prostate is allowed to breach 100\% of the prescription dose by a more significant amount.  This has created some difficulty in the comparison of different trial data as a 35Gy plan in one trial may be significantly different to a 35Gy plan in another.

SABR treatments typically have a far higher [[Biologically Effective Dose]] than radical treatments, typically greater than 100Gy (or even 150Gy), compared to radical treatments which are typically closer to the 60Gy range that we are actually delivering.  This makes SABR an ablative treatment replacing surgery rather than a cell-killing one.  Similar to [[Contact Radiotherapy]] or [[Brachytherapy]].
