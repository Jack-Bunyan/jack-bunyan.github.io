---
title: Stereotactic Radiosurgery
---
Stereotactic Radiosurgery(SRS), is a technique using a 3D arrangement of fields to treat a patient in a single fraction.  Rather than replying on a clinical window this ablates the tumour, equally killing both tumour and tissue in the target region.  This makes conformal [[Planning-UN 13-10-2026|Planning]] very important.  This means that the accuracy of the components are far more important, couch rotations become vital.

Whereas VMAT treatments are typically normalised to 100% being the mean dose to the PTV, this is instead set to 50-80% of the dose in SRS, this is because the falloff is so fast that very little of the PTV would be recieving the pescription.


SRS is split into 4 separate tiers.

## Tier 1
[[Brain]] Mets, these are approximately 70% of CCC SRS, and are the largest cohort of SRS patients.  May be being treated for single or many mets, with a maximum tumour volume of 20cc.

These patients will recieve between 15 and 24Gy.  Compared to whole brain they will have a similar survival rate, however improved side effects ,with 60% rather than 80% of patients having cognition loss at 6 months.  All patients receiving this are palliative, though it is likely that the SRS will treat their brain mets, most patients will die from systemic disease.

Chosen for Karnofsky >=70, with best case scenario being seen for lung or breast mets.

Benign Meningiomas (non-skull base), treated with 12-14Gy.

## Tier 2
Vestibular/Aucoustic Scwannoma, benign and slow growing tumour within the auditory canal, treated with between 12 and 14Gy.  Also skull-base meningiomas, these are slightly more complex than non-skull base meningiomas due to the number of nerve connections

## Tier 3
Arteriovenous Malformation (AVM), treated with 16-25Gy and mostly young adults from 20-40yo; these are locations where an artery is feeding blood to a vein.  Symptons are most common when they repture; as the vein is not meant to handle as high a pressure.

## Tier 4
Trigeminal Neuralgia, sever chronic facial pain disorder.  70-90Gy 4mm from the brainstem, this is intended to dampen the nerve signals, reducing the amount of pain experienced by the patient.  Patients are typically older than 50, and mostly women.

# Immobilisation
Traditionall SRS was done by screwing a frame into the skull of the patient, however this is invasive, and means that the entire process must be done in single day.  More modern systems are likely to use thermoplastic masks, however these allow for 1mm movements.  This does however allow for hyperfractionated treatments.  Open masks are allowing the introduction of [[SGRT]] during these treatments for live position analysis.

# Gamma knife
192 cobalt-60 sources, while historically these did not allow for additional imaging of the patient, newer systems can also include CBCT imaging.  High spatial accuracy and great collimation, however can only be used for intracranial targets.  And the delivery can take 5-15 minutes per target that is being treated.  

# Cyber knife
this is a 6MV linac that is mounted on a robotic arm.  This means that it has complete freedom of angle to deliver a pencil beam.  This can be adapted to either use a micro-MLC system, or a variable collimator.  Typically has sub-mm accuracy and has advanced kV imaging.  This is done live and the system matches patient motion during the treatment.  Typicalyl have quite long treatment times.

# Linacs
Typical linac system but using more advanced MLC system, with variable beam energies such as 6FFF or 10FFF.  A single arc that is being used can treat multiple mets, and using a 6DoF couch, arcs can be delivered from different angles.  These VMAT treatments can be much faster with treatment times between 5 and 10 minutes; however, are typically less conformal than other treatment methods.  Although the couch can rotate it typically doesn't make use of this for practicality limitations.

# QA
Typically measurements are done with diodes as they are so much smaller so get a more accurate measurement, with lass of a volume averaging affect.  If you hate yourself you could use film but this is slow.  Small fields are just a pain in the arse for dosimetry.
- Lateral electron disequilibrium
- Spectral Differences
- Source Occlusion

Tests:
- Winston Lutz
- off-axis tests
- kV/MV coincidence
- Couch rotation accuracy
- MLC position accuracy-more important for small fields

# Imaging
Very typical that [[MRI]] is used given how it is in the brain.  typically a T1+contrast sequence, plus T2.  AVMs are likely to use Angiography, typically fused with a [[CT]] imge to calculate the dose distribution.

During Treatment they will recieve a 3D [[CT#CBCT|CBCT]], with [[Planar X-ray]] being used for live imaging to ensure the patints position remains constant.
